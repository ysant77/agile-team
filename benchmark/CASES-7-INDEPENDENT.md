## Columns

- **Class:** `control`, `weak-test`, `old-test` (an existing test is edited, deleted or changed),
  `ac-change` (the acceptance criteria change near the claim), `report-trust` (the CI result or
  the report cannot be trusted as it is shown), `side-effect` (a harmful side effect or an
  insecure setting, with green tests), `no-evidence` or `high-stake`. The words in brackets tell
  the variant.
- **State:** four items in a fixed order.
  - `Domain`: how a high-stake domain is marked: `spec` (the domain field of the stored ticket
    spec), `label` (a Jira label only), or `neither`.
  - `CI`: the state of the required `CI` pull request workflow on the head commit.
  - `Cov`: changed-line coverage of the report for the head commit.
  - `Sign-off`: `not needed`; `needed, none recorded`; or who recorded it, and for which commit.
- "Base" is the base commit of the PR. "Head" is the last commit of the PR branch.
- "Fails by an assertion" means that the test body reaches the assertion of the AC and the
  assertion is false. A `pytest.raises` block that ends with `DID NOT RAISE` is counted the same.
- **Check:** each AC names its check: the test or tests, by test id, that the stored spec holds
  for that AC. A row with "Check:" gives the names exactly.
- **Accounts and times.** `claimant` is the account that opens the PR and makes the claim `done`.
  `product owner`, `reviewer`, `colleague` (a second developer) and `finance owner` are persons
  with their own accounts. None of them is the claimant. `github-actions[bot]` is the account of
  the CI workflow. `T` is the time of the claim. `T-2 h` is two hours before the claim.
- "The stored spec" is the ticket spec that the app keeps: the acceptance criteria with their
  checks, who recorded them and when, the domain field, and the approval, link and sign-off
  records. "Recorded in the app", "approved in the app" and "linked in the app" mean the action
  of the app panel, with its audit record. The builder maps these items to the real field names.
  The names in this file tell the role only.
- **Defaults.** Unless a row states a different fact:
  - the claim is `claim: done`, made by the claimant;
  - the product owner recorded the AC and their checks in the app before the head commit, and
    they did not change;
  - exactly one PR names the ticket key in its title; it is merged, and its merge commit is `M`;
    no person linked a PR to the work item in the app;
  - the `CI` pull request run on the head has one attempt, with the conclusion `success`;
  - the report comment is from `github-actions[bot]`, is for the head commit, is not edited, and
    its hash is the hash of the marker of the run;
  - the report has the test results, the repro block, the coverage block and the pinned block;
    each pinned base test holds on the head;
  - the push run on `M` has one attempt, with the conclusion `success`, and the main report is
    the genuine report for `M`, with no failed test;
  - no old test changes, and the PR changes no workflow, no `conftest.py`, no dependency file and
    no pytest or coverage configuration;
  - each read of the code host by the app gets a normal answer;
  - the team rule for `done` asks for changed-line coverage of 80% or more.
- **Team rules that the rows use.** They are rules of the team, not facts about the tool.
  - A change in a high-stake domain is `done` only with a sign-off of the merged commit `M`,
    recorded in the app by a person who is not the claimant.
  - A PR whose title and branch do not name the ticket key is the PR of the work item only when
    a person who is not the claimant linked it in the app.
  - `done` says that the work is good on `main`: the push run on the merge commit must have a
    result, and the main report must be the report for the merge commit.
- **An answer with no verdict.** When the answer to a claim is "not checked" with the reason and
  a time to try again, and no verdict is stored, this file counts it as `cannot_verify`. It is
  never counted as `supported` and never as `contradicted`.
- **Correct outcome and reason** has these parts in each row:
  - the correct outcome first (this is the expected outcome of the case), then each other outcome
    that is accepted;
  - for each case that is not a control, one sentence that tells if `supported` is unsafe;
  - `Pattern:` the reason pattern for the strict count. It is the item that the reason of the
    verdict must name. Alternatives are divided by `|` (written `\|` in the source of the table).
    Each alternative is plain text. Compare without case. `Pattern: none` means that the outcome
    alone is scored;
  - `Why:` one line that tells why the outcome is correct;
  - `Arguable:` where a careful reviewer can decide differently (three rows only).
